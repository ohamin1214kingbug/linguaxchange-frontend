A1 is where Portuguese stops being a fast, nasal blur and becomes a language you can get by in. You are aiming to handle predictable, everyday situations with someone who speaks slowly and is happy to help. This guide follows Brazilian Portuguese.

## What you'll be able to do | short: I can… | kicker: Your goal
Treat this as the real test of the level: grammar you can't use in these situations isn't learned yet.

- [ ] Introduce yourself and ask someone their name and where they're from
- [ ] Spell your name and give your phone number
- [ ] Talk about your family and where you live
- [ ] Order at a bakery or restaurant and ask the price
- [ ] Ask for directions and understand simple answers
- [ ] Say what you like and what you do every day
- [ ] Understand slow, clear speech on these topics

## Sounds and spelling | short: Sounds | kicker: Start here
Brazilian Portuguese has a few sounds that look different from how they're written. Learn these and most words fall into place.

| Letters | How it sounds | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ão, õe | nasal: air through the nose | pão, limões // bread, lemons |
| lh | like "lli" in million | filho // son |
| nh | "ny" as in canyon | vinho // wine |
| ç | "s" | açúcar // sugar |
| r at the start, rr | like an English "h" | Rio, carro // Rio, car |
| final -o | like "u" | obrigado // thank you |
| t, d before i | "ch", "j" in most of Brazil | tia, dia // aunt, day |

:::tip
A tilde (ã, õ) or an m / n after a vowel makes it nasal. Practise *pão*, *mãe* and *bom* slowly: they're among the first words you'll hear.
:::

:::mistake
- Rio with a rolled r => Rio sounds like "Hee-u"
At the start of a word, r sounds like an English h in most of Brazil.
:::

## Articles and contractions | short: Articles | kicker: Grammar · 1 of 4
Every noun is masculine or feminine. The articles also merge with small words like *em* (in) and *de* (of, from).

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| the | o livro, os livros | a casa, as casas |
| a / some | um livro, uns livros | uma casa, umas casas |
| em + the (in the) | no, nos | na, nas |
| de + the (of the) | do, dos | da, das |

:::examples
- O livro está **na** mesa. // The book is on the table.
- Eu sou **do** Brasil. // I'm from Brazil.
- Moro **no** Rio. // I live in Rio.
:::

:::tip
Learn every noun with its article: not "dia" but **o dia**. Most -o words are masculine and -a words feminine, but not all.
:::

:::mistake
- em o Brasil => no Brasil
- de a Maria => da Maria
Em and de always merge with the article.
:::

:::practice
1. ___ casa (the) = a
2. Eu moro ___ Brasil. (in the) = no
3. O carro ___ Ana. (of the) = da
4. Eu tenho ___ irmão. (a) = um
:::

## Ser and estar | short: Ser, estar | kicker: Grammar · 2 of 4
Like Spanish, Portuguese has two verbs for "to be".

| | Use it for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ser | who or what something **is**: name, origin, job | Eu sou a Ana. Sou de Lisboa. // I'm Ana. I'm from Lisbon. |
| estar | **how** something is right now, or where a person is | Estou cansada. Estou em casa. // I'm tired. I'm at home. |
| ficar | where a **place** is | Onde fica o banheiro? // Where's the bathroom? |

:::examples
- Meu irmão **é** médico. // My brother is a doctor.
- A Maria **está** no trabalho. // Maria is at work.
- O hotel **fica** perto da praia. // The hotel is near the beach.
:::

:::tip
In Brazil, the location of buildings and cities is usually said with **ficar**, not estar: *Onde fica a estação?*
:::

:::mistake
- Eu estou professor. => Eu sou professor.
A job is part of who you are, so it takes ser.
:::

:::practice
1. Eu ___ brasileira. (ser) = sou
2. Hoje eu ___ muito cansado. (estar) = estou
3. Onde ___ o museu? (ficar) = fica
:::

## The present tense | short: Present tense | kicker: Grammar · 3 of 4
In Brazil, *você* (you) uses the same verb form as *ele* and *ela*, so there are only four forms to learn.

| | falar (speak) | comer (eat) | morar (live) |
|---|---|---|---|
| eu | falo | como | moro |
| você / ele / ela | fala | come | mora |
| nós | falamos | comemos | moramos |
| vocês / eles / elas | falam | comem | moram |

A few everyday verbs are irregular: **tenho** (I have), **vou** (I go), **faço** (I do), **quero** (I want), **posso** (I can).

:::examples
- Eu moro em São Paulo e trabalho num banco. // I live in São Paulo and work at a bank.
- Você fala inglês? // Do you speak English?
- A gente come às oito. // We eat at eight.
:::

:::tip
In everyday Brazilian speech, **a gente** often replaces *nós* for "we", and it takes the você form: *a gente fala*, not *a gente falamos*.
:::

:::mistake
- Eu sou 20 anos. => Eu tenho 20 anos.
Age is something you "have" in Portuguese.
:::

:::practice
1. Eu ___ em Recife. (morar) = moro
2. Você ___ espanhol? (falar) = fala
3. Nós ___ às sete. (comer) = comemos
4. Eu ___ dois irmãos. (ter) = tenho
:::

## Questions and likes | short: Questions | kicker: Grammar · 4 of 4
A yes/no question is a statement with a rising tone: *Você fala inglês?* To say what you like, use **gostar de**.

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| o que | what | O que você faz? // What do you do? |
| quem | who | Quem é? // Who is it? |
| onde | where | Onde você mora? // Where do you live? |
| quando | when | Quando é a aula? // When is the class? |
| como | how | Como você se chama? // What's your name? |
| quanto | how much | Quanto custa? // How much is it? |
| por que | why | Por que você estuda português? // Why do you study Portuguese? |

:::examples
- Eu gosto **de** café. // I like coffee.
- Eu gosto **do** Brasil. // I like Brazil. (de + o = do)
- Você gosta **de** dançar? // Do you like dancing?
:::

:::mistake
- Eu gosto café. => Eu gosto de café.
Gostar always needs de after it.
:::

:::practice
1. ___ você mora? = Onde
2. ___ custa o ingresso? = Quanto
3. Eu gosto ___ música. (of the) = da
:::

## Your first conversation | short: Conversation | kicker: Put it together
Ordering at a Brazilian bakery (padaria), using almost everything above.

:::dialogue
- Atendente: Oi, tudo bem? O que vai ser? // Hi, how are you? What will it be?
- Lucas: Tudo bem! Um café e um pão de queijo, por favor. // Good! A coffee and a cheese bread, please.
- Atendente: Mais alguma coisa? // Anything else?
- Lucas: Não, obrigado. Quanto é? // No, thanks. How much is it?
- Atendente: Doze reais. // Twelve reais.
- Lucas: Aceita cartão? // Do you take cards?
- Atendente: Aceito, sim. // Yes, I do.
- Lucas: Valeu, tchau! // Thanks, bye!
:::

## Watch out for | short: Watch out | kicker: Common difficulties
**Obrigado or obrigada?** It agrees with the speaker, not the listener: men say *obrigado*, women say *obrigada*.

**Spanish speakers, careful.** Portuguese looks close to Spanish, which helps with reading, but the sounds and many everyday words are different. Don't assume a word is the same.

:::mistake
- Estou constipado. (meaning "I have a cold") => Estou resfriado.
In Brazil, constipado means constipated. Other false friends: puxar means pull (on doors), and esquisito means strange, not exquisite.
:::
