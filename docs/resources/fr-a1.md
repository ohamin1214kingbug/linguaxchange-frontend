A1 is where French stops being a blur of silent letters and becomes a language you can get by in. You are aiming to handle predictable, everyday situations with someone who speaks slowly and is happy to help.

## What you'll be able to do | short: I can… | kicker: Your goal
Treat this as the real test of the level: grammar you can't use in these situations isn't learned yet.

- [ ] Greet people politely and introduce yourself
- [ ] Spell your name and give your phone number
- [ ] Talk about your family and where you live
- [ ] Order in a café or bakery and ask the price
- [ ] Ask for directions and understand simple answers
- [ ] Say what you like and what you do every day
- [ ] Understand slow, clear speech on these topics

## Sounds and spelling | short: Sounds | kicker: Start here
French spelling looks hard because many letters are silent, but the rules are consistent.

| Letters | How it sounds | Example |
|---|---|---|
| final consonants | usually silent | petit, Paris // small, Paris |
| é | like "ay" in say | café // coffee |
| è, ê | like "e" in bed | mère, fête // mother, party |
| ou | "oo" as in food | vous // you |
| u | say "ee", round your lips | tu // you |
| an, on, in | nasal: air through the nose | blanc, bon, pain // white, good, bread |
| r | from the back of the throat | rue // street |

:::tip
When a word ending in a silent consonant is followed by a vowel, the consonant is often pronounced to link them: *vous avez* sounds like "voo-za-vay". This is called liaison.
:::

:::mistake
- Pronouncing the s in Paris => Paris sounds like "Pa-ree"
Most final consonants are silent. The usual exceptions are the letters in CaReFuL: c, r, f and l (avec, bonjour, neuf, il).
:::

## Articles and gender | short: Articles | kicker: Grammar · 1 of 4
Every noun is masculine or feminine, and the article tells you which. In front of a vowel, le and la shorten to l'.

| | Masculine | Feminine | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| the | le livre | la maison | les livres |
| before a vowel | l'ami | l'école | les amis |
| a / some | un livre | une maison | des livres |

:::examples
- Le café est chaud. // The coffee is hot.
- J'ai **une** sœur et **un** frère. // I have a sister and a brother.
- **Les** enfants sont à l'école. // The children are at school.
:::

:::tip
Learn every noun with its article: not "problème" but **le problème**. The ending often helps (-tion is feminine, -ment is masculine), but there are many exceptions.
:::

:::mistake
- la problème => le problème
Many words ending in -e are masculine. Learning the article with the word saves you guessing.
:::

:::practice
1. ___ maison (the) = la
2. ___ ami (the) = l'
3. ___ livres (the) = les
4. J'ai ___ chien. (a) = un
:::

## Être, avoir and -er verbs | short: Present tense | kicker: Grammar · 2 of 4
*Être* (to be) and *avoir* (to have) are irregular and appear in almost every sentence. Most other verbs end in -er and follow one pattern.

| | être (be) | avoir (have) | parler (speak) |
|---|---|---|---|
| je / j' | suis | ai | parle |
| tu | es | as | parles |
| il / elle / on | est | a | parle |
| nous | sommes | avons | parlons |
| vous | êtes | avez | parlez |
| ils / elles | sont | ont | parlent |

:::examples
- Je suis étudiante. J'habite à Lyon. // I'm a student. I live in Lyon.
- Tu parles anglais ? // Do you speak English?
- On a un chat. // We have a cat.
:::

:::tip
Use **tu** with friends, family and other students, and **vous** with strangers, in shops and with officials. *On* is the everyday way to say "we": *on mange à huit heures*.
:::

:::mistake
- Je suis 20 ans. => J'ai 20 ans.
In French you "have" your age.
:::

:::practice
1. Je ___ fatigué. (être) = suis
2. Nous ___ un chien. (avoir) = avons
3. Vous ___ français ? (parler) = parlez
4. Elles ___ à Paris. (être) = sont
:::

## Asking questions | short: Questions | kicker: Grammar · 3 of 4
The easiest question is a statement with a rising tone: *Tu viens ?* Adding **est-ce que** at the start works for any question.

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| qu'est-ce que | what | Qu'est-ce que tu fais ? // What are you doing? |
| qui | who | Qui est-ce ? // Who is it? |
| où | where | Où est la gare ? // Where is the station? |
| quand | when | Quand est le cours ? // When is the class? |
| comment | how | Comment tu t'appelles ? // What's your name? |
| combien | how much | C'est combien ? // How much is it? |
| pourquoi | why | Pourquoi tu apprends le français ? // Why are you learning French? |

:::mistake
- Combien coûte ? => Combien ça coûte ?
The verb needs a subject: ça (it) or the thing you're asking about.
:::

:::practice
1. ___ est la gare ? (where) = Où
2. ___ tu t'appelles ? (how) = Comment
3. ___ tu habites à Paris ? (est-ce que question) = Est-ce que
:::

## Saying no and what you like | short: No and likes | kicker: Grammar · 4 of 4
To say "not", wrap the verb in **ne … pas**. Before a vowel, ne becomes n'. With *aimer* (to like), French keeps the article: *j'aime le café*.

:::examples
- Je **ne** parle **pas** allemand. // I don't speak German.
- Elle **n'**habite **pas** ici. // She doesn't live here.
- J'aime **la** musique. // I like music.
- Je n'aime pas **le** fromage. // I don't like cheese.
:::

:::mistake
- J'aime café. => J'aime le café.
French uses le, la or les after aimer, even when English uses no article.
:::

:::practice
1. Je ne parle ___ anglais. = pas
2. J'aime ___ musique. = la
3. Il ___ habite pas ici. = n'
:::

## Your first conversation | short: Conversation | kicker: Put it together
Ordering in a café, using almost everything above.

:::dialogue
- Serveur: Bonjour ! Qu'est-ce que vous prenez ? // Hello! What will you have?
- Léa: Un café, s'il vous plaît. // A coffee, please.
- Serveur: Et avec ceci ? // Anything else?
- Léa: Un croissant aussi. // A croissant too.
- Serveur: Très bien. // Very good.
- Léa: Ça fait combien ? // How much is that?
- Serveur: Quatre euros cinquante. // Four euros fifty.
- Léa: Merci. Bonne journée ! // Thanks. Have a nice day!
:::

## Watch out for | short: Watch out | kicker: Common difficulties
**Listening.** Silent letters and liaison mean spoken French sounds very different from written French. Listen to short recordings and read along to connect the two.

**Gender.** You will get le and la wrong for a long time. Learning the article with every noun from day one is the only real shortcut.

:::mistake
- Je suis excité. (meaning "I'm excited") => J'ai hâte !
Excité can sound sexual in French. Other false friends: la librairie is a bookshop (a library is la bibliothèque), and actuellement means currently, not actually.
:::
