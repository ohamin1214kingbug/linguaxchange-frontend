TOPIK 2 is where your Korean stops being single short sentences. You can talk about what you did and will do, give reasons, say what you want and can do, and speak politely to people older than you.

## What you'll be able to do | short: I can… | kicker: Your goal
- [ ] Say what you did last weekend and what you'll do next
- [ ] Join two ideas with "and", "but" and "because"
- [ ] Say what you want, can and have to do
- [ ] Ask whether someone has ever tried something
- [ ] Make polite requests and speak respectfully to older people
- [ ] Use the phone, public transport and simple services

## Past and future | short: Past & future | kicker: Grammar · 1 of 5
The past adds **았/었** to the stem; the future (plans and guesses) uses **-(으)ㄹ 거예요**.

| Dictionary form | Past | Future |
|---|---|---|
| 가다 (go) | 갔어요 | 갈 거예요 |
| 먹다 (eat) | 먹었어요 | 먹을 거예요 |
| 마시다 (drink) | 마셨어요 | 마실 거예요 |
| 하다 (do) | 했어요 | 할 거예요 |
| 이다 (be) | 이었어요 / 였어요 | 일 거예요 |

:::examples
- 어제 친구를 **만났어요**. // I met a friend yesterday.
- 작년에 한국에 **갔어요**. // I went to Korea last year.
- 주말에 영화를 **볼 거예요**. // I'm going to watch a film at the weekend.
:::

:::mistake
- 어제 영화를 봐요. => 어제 영화를 봤어요.
Korean marks the past on the verb even when a time word like 어제 already says it.
:::

:::practice
1. 어제 커피를 ___. (마시다, past) = 마셨어요
2. 내일 공부___. (하다, future) = 할 거예요
3. 지난주에 부산에 ___. (가다, past) = 갔어요
:::

## Linking sentences | short: Linking | kicker: Grammar · 2 of 5
Instead of two short sentences, attach an ending to the first verb.

| Ending | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -고 | and | 밥을 먹고 커피를 마셔요. // I eat and then have coffee. |
| -지만 | but | 비싸지만 맛있어요. // It's expensive but tasty. |
| -아서 / -어서 | because, so | 피곤해서 일찍 잤어요. // I was tired, so I went to bed early. |
| -(으)니까 | because (with advice or requests) | 비가 오니까 우산을 가져가세요. // It's raining, so take an umbrella. |

:::tip
-아서/어서 never takes a past ending before it (피곤해서, not 피곤했어서) and can't be followed by a request or suggestion. For those, use -(으)니까.
:::

:::mistake
- 비가 와서 우산을 가져가세요. => 비가 오니까 우산을 가져가세요.
:::

:::practice
1. 날씨가 좋___ 산책했어요. (so) = 아서
2. 한국어는 어렵___ 재미있어요. (but) = 지만
3. 샤워하___ 잤어요. (and then) = 고
:::

## Wants, ability and must | short: Want & can | kicker: Grammar · 3 of 5
These endings attach to the verb stem and carry most of your everyday intentions.

| Ending | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -고 싶어요 | I want to | 한국에 가고 싶어요. // I want to go to Korea. |
| -(으)ㄹ 수 있어요 / 없어요 | can / can't | 운전할 수 있어요. // I can drive. |
| -아야 / -어야 해요 | have to | 내일 일찍 일어나야 해요. // I have to get up early tomorrow. |
| -(으)ㄹ게요 | I'll (a promise) | 제가 전화할게요. // I'll call. |

:::mistake
- 저는 한국에 가고 싶어해요. => 저는 한국에 가고 싶어요.
-고 싶어하다 is for other people's wishes: 동생이 가고 싶어해요.
:::

:::practice
1. 저는 김치를 먹을 수 ___. (can) = 있어요
2. 숙제를 해___ 해요. (have to) = 야
3. 새 옷을 사고 ___. (want) = 싶어요
:::

## Experience and "-ing" | short: Experience | kicker: Grammar · 4 of 5
| Ending | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -고 있어요 | be doing right now | 지금 숙제를 하고 있어요. // I'm doing homework now. |
| -아 / -어 보세요 | try (doing) | 이거 먹어 보세요. // Try this. |
| -(으)ㄴ 적이 있어요 | have ever done | 한국에 가 본 적이 있어요? // Have you ever been to Korea? |

:::examples
- 김치를 **먹어 본 적이 있어요**. // I've tried kimchi before.
- 친구를 **기다리고 있어요**. // I'm waiting for a friend.
:::

:::mistake
- 한국 음식을 먹는 적이 있어요. => 한국 음식을 먹은 적이 있어요.
Experience uses the past modifier -(으)ㄴ.
:::

:::practice
1. 지금 밥을 먹___ 있어요. = 고
2. 이 노래를 들어 ___. (try) = 보세요
3. 제주도에 가 본 적이 ___? (have you ever) = 있어요
:::

## Polite requests and honorifics | short: Honorifics | kicker: Grammar · 5 of 5
With older people, teachers and customers, raise the person you talk about with **-(으)세요** and a few special verbs. Ask for favours with **-아/어 주세요**.

| Plain | Honorific | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 있어요 | 계세요 | is (somewhere) |
| 먹어요 | 드세요 | eats |
| 자요 | 주무세요 | sleeps |
| 줘요 | 드려요 | gives (to someone senior) |
| 이름 / 나이 | 성함 / 연세 | name / age |

:::examples
- 천천히 **말해 주세요**. // Please speak slowly.
- 할머니께서 **주무세요**. // Grandma is sleeping.
- 선생님께 선물을 **드렸어요**. // I gave my teacher a present.
:::

:::tip
Never use honorifics about yourself: *제가 드세요* is wrong. They raise the other person, never the speaker.
:::

:::mistake
- 선생님한테 선물을 줬어요. => 선생님께 선물을 드렸어요.
:::

## Your weekend | short: Conversation | kicker: Put it together
:::dialogue
- 민수: 주말에 뭐 했어요? // What did you do at the weekend?
- 지은: 친구하고 부산에 갔어요. 정말 재미있었어요. // I went to Busan with a friend. It was really fun.
- 민수: 부산에서 뭐 먹었어요? // What did you eat in Busan?
- 지은: 회를 먹었어요. 먹어 본 적 있어요? // We had raw fish. Have you ever tried it?
- 민수: 아니요, 아직 없어요. 먹어 보고 싶어요. // No, not yet. I'd like to try it.
- 지은: 다음 달에 또 갈 거예요. 같이 갈래요? // I'm going again next month. Want to come?
- 민수: 좋아요! 그때는 시간이 있으니까 갈 수 있어요. // Sure! I'll have time then, so I can go.
:::

## Watch out for | short: Watch out | kicker: Common difficulties
**Irregular verbs** arrive all at once at this level. These four patterns cover most of them:

| Type | Dictionary form | Polite form |
|---|---|---|
| ㅂ | 덥다 (hot) | 더워요 |
| ㄷ | 듣다 (listen) | 들어요 |
| ㅡ | 바쁘다 (busy) | 바빠요 |
| 르 | 모르다 (not know) | 몰라요 |

**-고 있어요 vs -아/어 있어요.** *입고 있어요* is "putting on / wearing" as an action; *앉아 있어요* is "is seated", a state that continues. Learn the common ones as pairs.

:::mistake
- 날씨가 덥어요. => 날씨가 더워요.
덥다 is a ㅂ-irregular verb: the ㅂ turns into 우 before a vowel.
:::
