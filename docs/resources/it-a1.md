A1 is where Italian stops being music you enjoy and becomes a language you can get by in. You are aiming to handle predictable, everyday situations with someone who speaks slowly and is happy to help.

## What you'll be able to do | short: I can… | kicker: Your goal
Treat this as the real test of the level: grammar you can't use in these situations isn't learned yet.

- [ ] Introduce yourself and ask someone their name and where they're from
- [ ] Spell your name and give your phone number
- [ ] Talk about your family and where you live
- [ ] Order at a bar or restaurant and ask the price
- [ ] Ask for directions and understand simple answers
- [ ] Say what you like and what you do every day
- [ ] Understand slow, clear speech on these topics

## Sounds and spelling | short: Sounds | kicker: Start here
Italian is read almost exactly as it is written. A few letter pairs are worth learning first.

| Letters | How it sounds | Example |
|---|---|---|
| c, g before e / i | soft: "ch", "j" | ciao, gelato // hi, ice cream |
| ch, gh | hard: "k", "g" | che, spaghetti // what, spaghetti |
| gn | "ny" as in canyon | bagno // bathroom |
| gli | like "lli" in million | figlio // son |
| h | always silent | ho // I have |
| double letters | held a little longer | nonno // grandfather |

:::tip
Double consonants change the meaning: **nonno** is grandfather, **nono** is ninth; **palla** is a ball, **pala** is a shovel. Hold the double letter a moment longer.
:::

:::mistake
- Reading chiesa with an English "ch" => chiesa sounds like "kyeh-za"
In Italian, ch is always a hard "k". The soft "ch" sound is written c before e or i: cena, ciao.
:::

## Articles and gender | short: Articles | kicker: Grammar · 1 of 4
Every noun is masculine or feminine, and the article also depends on the first letter of the next word.

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| masculine (most) | il libro | i libri |
| masculine before s + consonant, z | lo studente | gli studenti |
| masculine before a vowel | l'amico | gli amici |
| feminine | la casa | le case |
| feminine before a vowel | l'amica | le amiche |

:::examples
- **Il** caffè è buono. // The coffee is good.
- Ho **un** fratello e **una** sorella. // I have a brother and a sister.
- **Gli** studenti sono in classe. // The students are in class.
:::

:::tip
Most nouns ending in -o are masculine and -a feminine. Plurals usually change -o to -i and -a to -e: libro → libri, casa → case.
:::

:::mistake
- il studente => lo studente
Masculine words starting with s + consonant or z take lo, not il.
:::

:::practice
1. ___ casa (the) = la
2. ___ libri (the) = i
3. ___ zaino (the) = lo
4. ___ amici (the) = gli
:::

## Essere, avere and -are verbs | short: Present tense | kicker: Grammar · 2 of 4
*Essere* (to be) and *avere* (to have) are irregular and used constantly. Most verbs end in -are and follow one pattern.

| | essere (be) | avere (have) | parlare (speak) |
|---|---|---|---|
| io | sono | ho | parlo |
| tu | sei | hai | parli |
| lui / lei | è | ha | parla |
| noi | siamo | abbiamo | parliamo |
| voi | siete | avete | parlate |
| loro | sono | hanno | parlano |

:::examples
- Sono Marco, sono di Roma. // I'm Marco, I'm from Rome.
- Parli inglese? // Do you speak English?
- Abbiamo una casa al mare. // We have a house by the sea.
:::

:::tip
The verb ending already says who is speaking, so Italians usually drop io and tu: *parlo italiano* is more natural than *io parlo italiano*. For formal "you", use **Lei** with the lui/lei form: *Lei parla inglese?*
:::

:::mistake
- Sono 20 anni. => Ho 20 anni.
In Italian you "have" your age.
:::

:::practice
1. Io ___ stanco. (essere) = sono
2. Noi ___ un cane. (avere) = abbiamo
3. Voi ___ italiano? (parlare) = parlate
4. Loro ___ di Milano. (essere) = sono
:::

## Questions and likes | short: Questions | kicker: Grammar · 3 of 4
A yes/no question is a statement with a rising tone: *Parli inglese?* To say what you like, Italian turns the sentence around: *mi piace* is literally "it pleases me".

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| che cosa | what | Che cosa fai? // What are you doing? |
| chi | who | Chi è? // Who is it? |
| dove | where | Dov'è la stazione? // Where is the station? |
| quando | when | Quando parte il treno? // When does the train leave? |
| come | how | Come ti chiami? // What's your name? |
| quanto | how much | Quanto costa? // How much is it? |
| perché | why | Perché studi italiano? // Why do you study Italian? |

:::examples
- Mi **piace** la pizza. // I like pizza. (one thing → piace)
- Mi **piacciono** i libri. // I like books. (more than one → piacciono)
:::

:::mistake
- Io piaccio la pizza. => Mi piace la pizza.
- Mi piace i libri. => Mi piacciono i libri.
:::

:::practice
1. ___ ti chiami? = Come
2. ___ costa il biglietto? = Quanto
3. Mi ___ il gelato. = piace
4. Mi ___ i gatti. = piacciono
:::

## Saying no, c'è and ci sono | short: No and there is | kicker: Grammar · 4 of 4
To say "not", put **non** in front of the verb. To say "there is", use **c'è** with one thing and **ci sono** with more than one.

:::examples
- **Non** parlo tedesco. // I don't speak German.
- **C'è** un bar qui vicino? // Is there a bar near here?
- **Ci sono** due camere. // There are two rooms.
:::

:::mistake
- C'è due bar. => Ci sono due bar.
:::

:::practice
1. ___ capisco. (I don't) = Non
2. ___ una farmacia qui? (there is) = C'è
3. ___ tre ragazzi. (there are) = Ci sono
:::

## Your first conversation | short: Conversation | kicker: Put it together
Ordering at an Italian bar, using almost everything above.

:::dialogue
- Barista: Buongiorno! Cosa prende? // Good morning! What will you have?
- Giulia: Un cappuccino e un cornetto, per favore. // A cappuccino and a croissant, please.
- Barista: Subito. Altro? // Right away. Anything else?
- Giulia: No, grazie. Quanto pago? // No, thanks. How much is it?
- Barista: Tre euro e venti. // Three euros twenty.
- Giulia: Ecco. Grazie, arrivederci! // Here you are. Thanks, goodbye!
:::

## Watch out for | short: Watch out | kicker: Common difficulties
**Articles.** Il, lo, l', i, gli: choosing the right one takes practice. Say whole phrases out loud (lo studente, gli amici) until they sound right.

**Double consonants.** Holding them too short can change the word. Exaggerate them at first.

:::mistake
- Ho una camera. (meaning "a camera") => Ho una macchina fotografica.
Camera means room. Other false friends: i parenti are relatives, not parents, and la libreria is a bookshop (a library is la biblioteca).
:::
