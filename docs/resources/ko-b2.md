TOPIK 4 is the level many Korean universities and employers ask for. You can follow the news, read articles and reports, speak formally at work, and express precise reasons, guesses and regrets.

## What you'll be able to do | short: I can… | kicker: Your goal
- [ ] Speak formally in meetings, presentations and interviews
- [ ] Read news articles and reports in the written -다 style
- [ ] Explain exactly why something happened, and whose fault it was
- [ ] Guess, hedge and talk about what would have happened
- [ ] Understand unfamiliar words from their Sino-Korean roots
- [ ] Discuss social topics: work, education, the environment

## Formal speech | short: Formal speech | kicker: Grammar · 1 of 5
At work, in presentations and in announcements, Koreans use the formal **-습니다 / -ㅂ니다** style, often with honorifics.

| Polite (요) | Formal | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 감사해요 | 감사합니다 | thank you |
| 있어요 | 있습니다 | there is |
| 할게요 | 하겠습니다 | I will do |
| 뭐예요? | 무엇입니까? | what is it? |
| 해 주세요 | 해 주십시오 | please do |

:::examples
- 회의를 **시작하겠습니다**. // Let's begin the meeting.
- 자료를 **보내 드리겠습니다**. // I'll send you the materials.
- 질문이 **있으십니까**? // Do you have any questions?
:::

:::tip
-겠- adds formality and intention: *하겠습니다* is the standard "I will" at work, and *잘 먹겠습니다* before a meal.
:::

:::mistake
- 제가 말씀하시겠습니다. => 제가 말씀드리겠습니다.
Never honour yourself: use the humble 드리다 / 말씀드리다 for your own actions.
:::

:::practice
1. 내일 다시 연락___. (I will, formal) = 드리겠습니다
2. 성함이 어떻게 ___? (formal question) = 되십니까
:::

## The written style | short: Written style | kicker: Grammar · 2 of 5
News, essays and reports use the plain **-다 / -ㄴ다 / -는다** endings and written connectors.

| Spoken | Written | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 가요 / 먹어요 | 간다 / 먹는다 | 학생들이 도서관에 간다. // Students go to the library. |
| 좋아요 | 좋다 | 날씨가 좋다. // The weather is good. |
| -고 | -(으)며 | 그는 교수이며 작가이다. // He is a professor and a writer. |
| -지만 | -(으)나 | 노력했으나 실패했다. // He tried, but failed. |
| -기 위해서 | -고자 | 문제를 해결하고자 한다. // We aim to solve the problem. |

:::mistake
- 나는 학생이에요다. => 나는 학생이다.
The -다 style replaces the 요 ending; it isn't added to it.
:::

:::practice
1. 그는 매일 운동을 ___. (하다, written) = 한다
2. 이 책은 재미있___. (written) = 다
:::

## Precise reasons | short: Reasons | kicker: Grammar · 3 of 5
At this level, reason endings carry nuance about blame and effort.

| Ending | Nuance | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -느라고 | because I was busy doing (an excuse) | 공부하느라고 전화를 못 받았어요. // I couldn't answer because I was studying. |
| -는 바람에 | because of (something unexpected, negative) | 비가 오는 바람에 경기가 취소됐어요. // The match was cancelled because of the rain. |
| -(으)ㄴ/는 덕분에 | thanks to (positive) | 친구가 도와준 덕분에 끝냈어요. // I finished thanks to my friend's help. |
| -(으)므로 | because (written, formal) | 위험하므로 출입을 금지한다. // Entry is prohibited as it is dangerous. |

:::mistake
- 친구가 도와준 바람에 끝냈어요. => 친구가 도와준 덕분에 끝냈어요.
-는 바람에 is for unexpected, usually negative causes.
:::

:::practice
1. 일하___ 점심을 못 먹었어요. (busy doing) = 느라고
2. 길이 막히___ 바람에 늦었어요. = 는
:::

## Guesses and regrets | short: Guesses | kicker: Grammar · 4 of 5
| Ending | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -(으)ㄹ 텐데 | it must / would be… (concern) | 많이 피곤할 텐데 좀 쉬세요. // You must be tired, so get some rest. |
| -(으)ㄹ 리가 없다 | there's no way… | 그럴 리가 없어요. // That can't be. |
| -았/었더라면 | if (only) I had… | 일찍 출발했더라면 안 늦었을 거예요. // If I'd left early, I wouldn't have been late. |
| -(으)ㄹ 뻔했다 | almost… | 넘어질 뻔했어요. // I almost fell over. |

:::practice
1. 지금 가면 길이 막힐 ___. (concern) = 텐데
2. 미리 공부했___ 합격했을 거예요. (if only) = 더라면
:::

## Words from roots | short: Sino-Korean | kicker: Grammar · 5 of 5
About 60% of Korean vocabulary comes from Chinese characters (한자어). Knowing common roots lets you guess new words.

| Root | Meaning | Words |
|---|---|---|
| 학 (學) | study | 학생 student, 대학 university, 학교 school |
| 생 (生) | life, birth | 생일 birthday, 학생 student, 생활 daily life |
| 회 (會) | meet | 회의 meeting, 회사 company, 사회 society |
| 문 (問) | ask | 질문 question, 문제 problem, 방문 visit |
| 동 (動) | move | 운동 exercise, 자동차 car, 활동 activity |

:::tip
When you meet a new word, look for a syllable you already know in another word. Many textbooks list the hanja next to new vocabulary for exactly this reason.
:::

## In a meeting | short: Conversation | kicker: Put it together
:::dialogue
- 팀장: 지난주 회의 결과를 말씀해 주시겠습니까? // Could you tell us the results of last week's meeting?
- 수진: 네, 말씀드리겠습니다. 일정이 바뀌는 바람에 발표가 다음 달로 미뤄졌습니다. // Yes. Because the schedule changed, the presentation has been postponed to next month.
- 팀장: 그럼 준비할 시간이 더 생겼네요. // Then we have more time to prepare.
- 수진: 네, 다만 예산이 부족할 텐데 미리 확인해야 할 것 같습니다. // Yes, but the budget may be short, so I think we should check in advance.
- 팀장: 좋은 지적입니다. 자료를 정리해서 보내 주십시오. // Good point. Please put the materials together and send them.
:::

## Watch out for | short: Watch out | kicker: Common difficulties
**Near-synonymous grammar.** -아서, -니까, -기 때문에, -느라고 and -는 바람에 all say "because". Learn each with a typical sentence and when it doesn't fit.

**Writing in speech.** Learners who study from textbooks start speaking in -다 or 합니다 style with friends. Keep the 요 form for daily conversation.

:::mistake
- 사장님께서 저한테 말했어요. => 사장님께서 저한테 말씀하셨어요.
When the subject is honoured with 께서, the verb needs honorifics too.
:::

## Ready for TOPIK 5? | short: Next | kicker: Next step
You're ready when you can switch between formal and polite speech without thinking, read a news article without stopping at every ending, and choose the right reason ending for the situation. TOPIK 4 is often asked for by Korean universities and employers, but requirements vary by institution and change: confirm them with the school or company.
