A1 is the point where Spanish stops being a foreign noise and becomes a language you can survive in. You are not aiming for fluency. You are aiming to handle predictable, everyday situations with a patient speaker who is willing to slow down.

## What you'll be able to do | short: I can… | kicker: Your goal
Treat this as the real test of the level: grammar you can't use in these situations isn't learned yet.

- [ ] Introduce yourself and ask someone their name, age and where they're from
- [ ] Spell your name aloud and understand a spelled name
- [ ] Describe your family and where you live
- [ ] Say what you do for work or study
- [ ] Order food and drink, and ask what something costs
- [ ] Ask for simple directions and follow them
- [ ] Talk about your day and what you like
- [ ] Understand slow, clear speech on these topics

## Sounds and spelling | short: Sounds | kicker: Start here
Spanish is written almost exactly as it sounds. Learn these few rules once and you can read any word aloud.

| Letter | How it sounds | Example |
|---|---|---|
| h | always silent | hola // hello |
| ñ | like "ny" in canyon | niño // child |
| ll, y | like "y" in yes | me llamo // my name is |
| j, g before e/i | a breathy "h" | jamón, gente // ham, people |
| c before e/i, z | "th" in Spain, "s" in Latin America | cena, zumo // dinner, juice |
| rr | a rolled r | perro // dog |

:::tip
The written accent shows where the stress falls, and it can change the meaning: **está** (is) and **esta** (this) are different words.
:::

:::mistake
- Pronouncing the h in hola => hola sounds like "ola"
The letter h is never pronounced in Spanish.
:::

## Nouns and articles | short: Nouns | kicker: Grammar · 1 of 4
Every Spanish noun is masculine or feminine, and the words around it change to match. This touches every sentence you will say, so build the habit early.

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| the (one) | el libro // the book | la casa // the house |
| the (many) | los libros // the books | las casas // the houses |
| a / some | un libro, unos libros | una casa, unas casas |

:::examples
- Un café **pequeño**, por favor. // A small coffee, please.
- La casa es **bonita**. // The house is pretty.
- Mis amigos son **simpáticos**. // My friends are nice.
:::

:::tip
Learn every noun with its article: not "problema" but **el problema**. Most words ending in -o are masculine and in -a feminine, but not all.
:::

:::mistake
- la problema => el problema
- el mano => la mano
A few common words break the -o / -a pattern. Learning the article with the word saves you from guessing.
:::

:::practice
1. ___ libro (the) = el
2. ___ casas (the) = las
3. Un coche ___ (red, rojo/roja) = rojo
4. Una mesa ___ (small, pequeño/pequeña) = pequeña
:::

## Ser, estar and hay | short: Ser, estar, hay | kicker: Grammar · 2 of 4
English has one verb, "to be". Spanish splits it into three tools with different jobs. Learn them as three tools, not three translations.

| | Use it for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ser | who or what something **is**: name, origin, job, the time | Soy Ana. Soy de Madrid. // I'm Ana. I'm from Madrid. |
| estar | **where** something is, or **how** it is right now | Estoy en casa. Estoy cansada. // I'm at home. I'm tired. |
| hay | that something **exists**: "there is / there are" | Hay un banco cerca. // There's a bank nearby. |

:::examples
- Mi hermano **es** médico. // My brother is a doctor. (job → ser)
- ¿Dónde **está** el baño? // Where is the bathroom? (location → estar)
- ¿**Hay** agua? // Is there any water? (exists? → hay)
:::

:::tip
Ask yourself one question: **what is it** (ser), **where or how is it now** (estar), or **does it exist** (hay)?
:::

:::mistake
- Estoy profesor. => Soy profesor.
A job is part of who you are, so it takes ser, even if you change jobs next year.
:::

:::practice
1. Mi hermano ___ médico. = es
2. ¿Dónde ___ la estación? = está
3. ___ dos cafés en la mesa. = Hay
4. Hoy ___ muy cansada. = estoy
:::

## The present tense | short: Present tense | kicker: Grammar · 3 of 4
At A1 the present tense does almost all the work, even for plans: *mañana voy a Madrid* means "I'm going to Madrid tomorrow".

| | hablar (speak) | comer (eat) | vivir (live) |
|---|---|---|---|
| yo | hablo | como | vivo |
| tú | hablas | comes | vives |
| él / ella / usted | habla | come | vive |
| nosotros / nosotras | hablamos | comemos | vivimos |
| vosotros / vosotras | habláis | coméis | vivís |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | hablan | comen | viven |

**Vosotros** ("you all", informal) is used in Spain. In Latin America everyone says **ustedes** instead, with the same ending as *ellos*: *ustedes hablan*. Learn the one for the Spanish you'll hear most.

A handful of everyday verbs are irregular, and you will need them from day one: **tengo** (I have), **voy** (I go), **hago** (I do), **quiero** (I want), **puedo** (I can).

:::examples
- Vivo en Madrid y trabajo en un hotel. // I live in Madrid and work in a hotel.
- ¿Hablas inglés? // Do you speak English?
- Quiero un café, por favor. // I'd like a coffee, please.
:::

:::tip
The verb ending already says who is doing it, so Spanish speakers usually drop **yo** and **tú**. *Hablo español* is more natural than *yo hablo español*.
:::

:::mistake
- Soy 20 años. => Tengo 20 años.
Age is something you "have" in Spanish, not something you "are".
:::

:::practice
1. Yo ___ en Barcelona. (vivir) = vivo
2. ¿Tú ___ español? (hablar) = hablas
3. Nosotros ___ a las dos. (comer) = comemos
4. ¿Vosotros ___ en Madrid? (vivir) = vivís
5. Yo ___ dos hermanos. (tener) = tengo
:::

## Questions and likes | short: Questions | kicker: Grammar · 4 of 4
Questions are mostly about a few question words and your tone of voice. A yes/no question is just a statement with a rising tone: *¿Hablas inglés?*

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| qué | what | ¿Qué quieres? // What do you want? |
| dónde | where | ¿Dónde vives? // Where do you live? |
| cuándo | when | ¿Cuándo es la clase? // When is the class? |
| cuánto | how much | ¿Cuánto cuesta? // How much is it? |
| cómo | how | ¿Cómo te llamas? // What's your name? |
| por qué | why | ¿Por qué estudias español? // Why do you study Spanish? |

To say what you like, Spanish turns the sentence around: *me gusta el café* is literally "coffee pleases me". The thing you like decides the verb.

:::examples
- Me **gusta** el café. // I like coffee. (one thing → gusta)
- Me **gustan** los libros. // I like books. (more than one → gustan)
- ¿Te gusta bailar? // Do you like dancing?
:::

:::mistake
- Yo gusto el café. => Me gusta el café.
- Me gusta los libros. => Me gustan los libros.
:::

:::practice
1. ¿___ te llamas? = Cómo
2. ¿___ cuesta el billete? = Cuánto
3. Me ___ la música. = gusta
4. Me ___ los perros. = gustan
:::

## Your first conversation | short: Conversation | kicker: Put it together
A short exchange in a café, using almost everything above.

:::dialogue
- Camarero: ¡Hola! ¿Qué quiere tomar? // Hi! What would you like?
- Ana: Un café con leche, por favor. // A white coffee, please.
- Camarero: ¿Algo más? // Anything else?
- Ana: Sí, ¿hay tortilla? // Yes, is there any tortilla?
- Camarero: Sí, claro. // Yes, of course.
- Ana: ¿Cuánto es? // How much is it?
- Camarero: Son cinco euros. // It's five euros.
- Ana: Gracias. ¡Adiós! // Thanks. Bye!
:::

## Watch out for | short: Watch out | kicker: Common difficulties
**Ser versus estar** stays uncomfortable for months. It isn't a rule you memorise once; it's a distinction you gradually stop translating.

**Agreement while speaking.** Most learners get gender right in writing long before they can do it in conversation. Only speaking practice fixes this.

:::mistake
- Estoy embarazada. (meaning "I'm embarrassed") => Me da vergüenza.
Embarazada means pregnant. Other false friends: éxito is success, not exit; actualmente means currently, not actually.
:::

## Ready for A2? | short: Next | kicker: Next step
You're ready when you can hold a slow, simple conversation about yourself and your surroundings without rehearsing it first, and the present tense feels automatic rather than assembled.

A1 often takes around 80 to 100 hours of guided study, but that varies widely with your experience and how much you speak. Treat it as a rough scale, not a target. Next up is the Spanish A2 guide, which opens the past tenses.
